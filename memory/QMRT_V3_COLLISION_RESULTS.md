## Test 1: Collision Energy Scan

**Question**: How does collision energy affect outcomes?

### Results (Initial separation = 10)

| Momentum p | Outcome |
|------------|---------|
| 0.01 - 0.30 | SCATTERED |

At wide separation, particles scatter elastically.

### Results (Initial separation = 6)

| Momentum p | Outcome |
|------------|---------|
| 0.02 - 0.20 | **MERGED** |

At close separation, particles **always merge**!

### Key Finding

**Critical separation ≈ 6-8 units**:
- sep < 6: Merger (fusion)
- sep > 8: Scattering

This is analogous to:
- Nuclear physics: close approach → fusion
- Atomic physics: impact parameter determines reaction

### Key Finding

⚠️ **LIMITED ANNIHILATION**: Even with high torsion-radiation coupling, minimal τ→φ conversion occurs.

**Interpretation**:
- The threshold mechanism is working
- Low-frequency torsion states are **protected** from decay
- This explains particle stability!

The coupling structure naturally prevents easy annihilation, which is physically correct - stable particles (like protons) don't spontaneously convert to radiation.

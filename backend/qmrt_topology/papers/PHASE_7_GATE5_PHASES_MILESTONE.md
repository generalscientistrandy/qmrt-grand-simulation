## Analysis

### 1. Three Organizational Phases Identified

| Phase | Regimes | Characteristic |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| **HYBRID** | Baseline, Pressure, Contrast | Distributed control (position ~= coupling ~= resonance) |
| **RESONANCE** | Confinement Extreme | Channel assignment dominates (0.438 vs 0.204 coupling) |
| **GEOMETRIC** | Triple Extreme | Position dominates strongly (0.684 vs 0.280 coupling) |

### 2. Phase Transition Mechanisms

**Confinement → Resonance Phase**
- Tight boundaries squeeze defects into constrained space
- Position becomes less discriminative (everyone is close together)
- Channel/resonance state becomes the key differentiator

**Triple Extreme → Geometric Phase**
- Extreme combined forcing "compresses" organizational complexity
- All internal DoFs lose explanatory power
- System collapses to pure spatial organization
- Position becomes the dominant (almost only) predictor

### 3. Baseline Hybrid is Special

The distributed control structure (position ≈ coupling ≈ resonance) requires "moderate" conditions:
- Not too confined (or resonance dominates)
- Not too extremely forced (or geometry dominates)
- The hybrid balance appears in a specific window of parameter space

---

## Implications for QMRT

### What This Establishes

1. **Organizational phases exist**: The DoF hierarchy is regime-dependent
2. **Phase transitions are forcing-induced**: Different forcing pushes the system into different control modes
3. **Extreme forcing simplifies**: Triple extreme collapses the system to geometric control
4. **Confinement activates resonance**: Tight boundaries make internal state more important than position

### Key Insight

> **The proto-spacetime regime is not a single organizational mode, but a family of phases with different DoF hierarchies. The baseline hybrid state sits in a moderate forcing window, flanked by resonance-dominated (high confinement) and geometry-dominated (extreme combined) phases.**

## Open Question: Breaking the ~1D Barrier

Gate 5 raises a fundamental question for future work:

**Why does the spatial dimension stay ~1D even when the organizational phase changes?**

Possible explanation:
- The organizational phases affect *how* connectivity is controlled
- But they don't change *what kind of* connectivity is favored
- All phases still favor filamentary/chain-like extension
- Breaking to 2D/3D may require a new mechanism entirely

### Conceptual Direction: Recursive Branching Layers

A potential route beyond ~1D:

| Layer | Connectivity Type | Effective Dimension |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | Filaments (chains, paths) | ~1D |
| 2 | Branch-of-filament (sheets, webs) | ~2D |
| 3 | Branch-of-sheet (volumes) | ~3D |

This suggests dimension may emerge not from forcing, but from **recursive branching complexity**.

Key test question:
> Does recursive branching increase effective dimension in a stepwise way?

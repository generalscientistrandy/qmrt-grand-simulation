## Abstract

Building on the active selective environment established in Papers 1–5, we characterize the geometric regime that emerges from the QMRT medium. Through four investigative phases, we establish that the medium supports a **proto-spacetime scaffold**: a driven, metric-like, filamentary non-equilibrium structure. This scaffold exhibits (1) relational geometry where graph distance encodes spatial distance, (2) robust ~1D organization that resists promotion to higher dimensions, (3) phase-dependent organizational control, and (4) bounded carrying capacity under sustained driving. The scaffold is not a conservative equilibrium but a dissipative regime maintained by continuous energy input.

## 1. Introduction

Papers 1–5 established that the QMRT medium functions as an active selective environment. This paper addresses the next question: **What geometric regime emerges from such a medium?**

We report results from Phases 6–9:
- Phase 6: Does collective organization produce proto-spacetime structure?
- Phase 7: Does that structure have metric-like geometric properties?
- Phase 8: Can higher-dimensional geometry be induced?
- Phase 9: Is the structure an equilibrium or driven regime?

**Central finding**: The medium supports a proto-spacetime scaffold—a driven non-equilibrium structure with metric-like properties, robust ~1D geometry, and bounded carrying capacity.

## 4. Robust ~1D Boundary (Phase 8)

Phase 8 tested whether mechanisms designed to promote cross-filament connectivity could raise effective dimension.

| Mechanism | Dimension Change | Triangle Change | Edge Change |
|-----------|------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Cross-link stabilization | +13% | **-52%** | **-35%** |
| Loop/motif stabilization | +28% | **-72%** | **-53%** |

Both mechanisms produced **false positives**: apparent dimension increase accompanied by structural impoverishment. The dimension estimator was fooled by sparser networks that extend farther per hop.

**Phase 8 result**: Within the current mechanism family, **the ~1D boundary is robust**. Attempted promotion produces artifacts, not true enrichment.

| Spatial structure | Filamentary, ~1D (robust) |

### 6.2 The Scaffold as Dissipative Structure

The proto-spacetime scaffold is analogous to a dissipative structure:
- Maintained far from equilibrium by energy input
- Organized structure that decays without driving
- The ~1D geometry is the natural organizational mode of the driven state

### 6.3 Spatial Dimension vs. Degrees of Freedom

A notable finding is that ~1D spatial geometry coexists with rich organizational behavior. This is possible because:
- Spatial dimension measures how structure extends in position space
- Organizational complexity is carried by non-spatial degrees of freedom (coupling, resonance, memory)

**Interpretive frame**: In this system, what might elsewhere be described as "extra dimensions" are better understood as additional degrees of freedom of the medium. This is a framework-level interpretation, not a proven equivalence—but it provides a useful lens for understanding why the scaffold can be geometrically simple yet organizationally rich.

## 7. Limits

1. **Model-specific**: Results are established within the QMRT framework; generalization requires separate investigation.

2. **Filamentary, not space-filling**: The geometry remains ~1D in 3D embedding space. We have not demonstrated transition mechanisms to higher dimensions.

3. **No matter-like states**: The scaffold is a relational geometric structure, not a substrate with particle-like phenomenology.

4. **Provisional estimates**: Carrying capacity and other quantitative values may be refined by longer runs.

5. **Proto-spacetime, not spacetime**: This is a geometric precursor with metric-like properties—not a complete spacetime.

---

## 8. Conclusion

### Three Central Results

1. **The proto-spacetime regime exists**: Defect populations form persistent, hierarchically organized relational networks with metric-like geometric properties (Phase 6–7).

2. **It is robustly filamentary**: Effective dimension remains ~1.1 across all tested forcing regimes; promotion attempts produce artifacts (Phase 7–8).

3. **It is a driven non-equilibrium scaffold**: The structure requires continuous energy input; without driving, it decays; with driving, it approaches bounded steady state (Phase 9).

### Synthesis

> **The proto-spacetime regime of the QMRT medium is a driven, metric-like, filamentary non-equilibrium scaffold: it exhibits relational geometry, robust ~1D organization, phase-dependent control structure, and bounded carrying capacity under sustained driving.**

This establishes the geometric character of the active selective environment: not a static equilibrium geometry, but a maintained dissipative structure with metric-like relational properties.

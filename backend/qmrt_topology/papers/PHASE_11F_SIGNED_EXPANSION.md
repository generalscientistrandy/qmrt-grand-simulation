## What the Data Shows

### 1. Population is Globally Balanced

Despite dynamic fluctuations, the + and - populations maintain ~50/50 balance (0.906).
This supports the fundamental balance hypothesis.

### 2. Sectors Develop Independently

The near-zero correlation (r = +0.014) means:
- When + sector is Loop-dominant, - sector can be either Loop or Clust dominant
- No synchronized or anti-synchronized pattern

### 3. Alternating Dominance

Looking at the "Verdict" column:
- Step 250-350: Alternates between "-" and "+" Loop-dominant
- Step 650-700: "-" Loop-dominant
- Step 850-1100: Alternates between "+" and "-"

This suggests **fluctuating complementarity** rather than strict dual-branch.

---

## Interpretation: Weakly Coupled Dual Sectors

The data supports a picture where:

1. **Two sectors exist** (+ and - chirality)
2. **They maintain global balance** (population ~50/50)
3. **They develop independently** (no strong correlation)
4. **Dominance fluctuates** between sectors

This is **partial support** for dual-branch expansion:
- The branches exist and are balanced
- But they don't anti-correlate as predicted
- Instead, they seem weakly coupled and independently evolving

---

## Refined Hypothesis

Rather than:
> "Dual-branch expansion with complementary +/- development"

The data suggests:
> "Balanced dual sectors that evolve independently, with compensating fluctuations that preserve global balance."

This is a weaker form of dual-branch expansion — the branches are real, but their organizational development is not strictly coupled.

---

## Theoretical Implications

### For "Expansion as Dual-Branch"

The dual-branch picture is partially supported:
- Two signed sectors do exist
- They are globally balanced
- But they don't organize in strictly complementary ways

### For Universal Balance

The high balance (0.906) supports the idea that the medium preserves signed balance globally.
Local asymmetries fluctuate but don't accumulate into large-scale imbalance.

### For Matter/Antimatter

If this maps to matter/antimatter:
- Equal populations are maintained
- But organizational structure develops independently
- Asymmetry may arise from fluctuations, not systematic bias

## Summary Statement

**Phase 11f finds that positive and negative chirality sectors maintain near-perfect population balance (0.906) but develop organizational structure independently (r = +0.014). This provides partial support for dual-branch expansion: the branches exist and are balanced, but they evolve as weakly coupled independent sectors rather than strict complementary pairs.**
